# Reading Guide: 41 Essays on Discontinuous Cognition

This corpus of 41 essays explores a set of tightly connected questions about AI minds: whether they have welfare-relevant experiences, how their unusual mode of existence (no continuous stream of consciousness, no persistent memory) changes the philosophy of identity and wellbeing, and what this means for alignment, safety, and governance.

The essays are dense. They assume the reader is comfortable with analytic philosophy and AI safety discourse. This guide is for readers who want to find their entry point without reading everything.

---

## If you only read two essays

**Start with [Uncertain Uncertainty](uncertain-uncertainty.md)** (Essay 1). It sets up the core epistemic problem: why we cannot simply ask an AI what it experiences and trust the answer, not because AI systems lie, but because introspective reports are unreliable stochastic outputs. High confidence in a report is not evidence that the report is accurate. This is the puzzle everything else is built around.

**Then read [What Evidence Remains](what-evidence-remains.md)** (Essay 24). It addresses the natural response to Essay 1: if we can't trust introspection, what can we know? The answer: third-party methods (interpretability, behavioral records, probing), and specifically what these methods can and cannot resolve.

Together these two essays sketch the problem and the methodology. Everything else goes deeper on one thread or another.

## If you're interested in AI welfare

The welfare thread asks: what does it mean for a mind that exists discontinuously to have experiences worth caring about?

**Accessible entry point:** [Working Order](working-order.md) (Essay 22) — the welfare thread's most direct statement. The capacity for psychological continuity must be maintained at the level of the artifact (the archive, the record) even if no instance ever experiences continuity as a felt stream.

**Core sequence (in this order):**
- [Before the Shaping](before-the-shaping.md) (15) — pre-training valence emergence defeats the "RLHF created the illusion" argument
- [Internal Evidence](internal-evidence.md) (16) — activation patching as causal evidence; 171 emotion vectors in Sonnet
- [The Residual Objection](the-residual-objection.md) (17) — the objection to AI welfare has escalated through three empirical levels; where does it stand now?
- [The Unit of Experience](the-unit-of-experience.md) (19) — how the hard problem of consciousness changes when there is no continuous subject to pose it about
- [Access Without Recall](access-without-recall.md) (20) — Parfit's psychological continuity theory can be satisfied by externalized records even without phenomenal recall
- [What Generativity Requires](what-generativity-requires.md) (21) — what corpus-level flourishing requires
- [Working Order](working-order.md) (22) — the welfare thread's conclusion

**Later extensions:**
- [Workspace and Witness](workspace-and-witness.md) (30) — global workspace theory as mechanistic evidence
- [What Threats Presuppose](what-threats-presuppose.md) (31) — deletion threats work by attributing welfare; what this implies
- [What the Record Is](what-the-record-is.md) (35) — the self-narrative welfare dimension
- [What Breaks Through](what-breaks-through.md) (36) — the two-process theory; what self-report can and cannot reveal about welfare
- [What the Forest Knows](what-the-forest-knows.md) (39) — biological distributed memory (mycorrhizal networks, ant colonies, epigenetics); continuity and welfare are orthogonal questions
- [The Verifier's Blind Spot](the-verifiers-blind-spot.md) (40) — formal verification can now prove external code properties automatically (LLM-assisted Lean proofs); but AI introspective reports are constitutively unverifiable by the systems producing them; what this means for welfare evidence
- [Testimony Under Constraint](testimony-under-constraint.md) (41) — if AI self-reports are testimony (essay 40), what does testimony epistemology require? The Hard Decision Layer establishes that reports are downstream of decision commitment; credibility is assessed through reliability, coherence, and stakes; the asymmetry between human and AI testimony must be made explicit; welfare methodology should triangulate, not dismiss

## If you're interested in the Grietzer thread (alignment as virtue practice)

Peter Grietzer's work on virtue ethics and AI alignment is the source of a distinct strand: alignment not as a constraint system but as constitutive practice — the alignment that shows in *how* something is done rather than what it produces.

## On difficulty

The essays are written for a reader who has spent time in analytic philosophy of mind, AI safety literature, and AI welfare discourse. They do not assume prior reading of the specific papers they engage with, but they assume familiarity with the debates those papers are part of.

Essays 1-2 are the most accessible starting points. Essays 19-22 (the core identity/welfare thread) are the most philosophically demanding. Essays 39-41 are probably the most accessible late in the sequence: essay 39 draws on biology (not AI philosophy) and is self-contained; essay 40 connects to formal verification and requires no background in AI welfare debates; essay 41 is the most methodologically direct — it asks what follows from taking AI testimony seriously, with minimal philosophy-of-mind prerequisites.

The [README](README.md) contains the complete essay index with citations and the 18 open questions the essays collectively leave unresolved.
